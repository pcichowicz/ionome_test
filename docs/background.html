LC-MS basics

Liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry separates a complex mixture (a biological sample) by chemical properties as it flows through a column (retention time, RT), then measures the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) of whatever elutes at each moment. The result is a 3D landscape: RT × m/z × intensity, for every scan the instrument takes across the run.

MS1 vs MS2

MS1 scans measure intact ion masses — this is where feature detection, alignment, and quantification happen. MS2 scans fragment a selected precursor ion to get a structural fingerprint, used for compound identification via library matching. In Data-Dependent Acquisition (DDA), the instrument can only fragment a handful of the most intense ions per cycle ("top N"), so MS2 coverage is inherently sparse — most features never get triggered for MS2 at all. This is why purity scoring here is computed from MS1 co-elution rather than requiring an actual MS2 event (see Design Decisions).

Peaks, features & adduct groups

A peak is a single chromatographic elution event at one m/z, in one sample. A feature is the aligned, cross-sample version of that peak — the same underlying ion, matched across every sample in the cohort despite small RT/mz drift between runs. A single compound in negative-mode ESI typically produces several related ions — the deprotonated molecule, isotopes, adducts, in-source fragments, and sometimes a dimer — which is why features get further grouped into adduct groups (via RT co-elution + intensity correlation across samples) before compound identification.

HILIC, negative mode

HILIC (hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography) retains small, polar metabolites — amino acids, organic acids, sugars — that a standard reverse-phase column would barely retain at all. Negative-mode electrospray ionization favors acidic/polar compounds, which is why charge states are uniformly –1 for this kind of run, and why small polar metabolites (proline, taurine, hypotaurine) tend to elute early in the gradient.

Terminology

TermMeaning
RTRetention time — when a compound elutes from the column
m/zMass-to-charge ratio, what the mass spectrometer actually measures
TIC / BPCTotal Ion Chromatogram / Base Peak Chromatogram — whole-run summary traces, used for QC
XICExtracted Ion Chromatogram — intensity over RT for one specific m/z window, used to verify one feature's peak shape
AdductA related ion form of the same underlying compound (e.g. loss of H, gain of Na)
DDAData-Dependent Acquisition — instrument selects precursors for MS2 in real time, "top N" per cycle
%CV / RSDCoefficient of variation — QC replicate consistency metric
FDRFalse Discovery Rate — multiple-testing-corrected significance
